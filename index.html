Our schools have a story.
Help your neighbors hear it.

This November, the people who live in the Littleton Public Schools district will vote on a $10 million mill levy override. The campaign lives at citizensforlps.org. That is where you sign up to volunteer (the form has a box for "I will take a yard sign," and signs go out starting September 12) and where the event calendar is. This site does one thing. It helps you tell your own story about these schools, in your own words, to the people who already know you.

The district is holding seven public forums this fall. Superintendent Todd Lambert walks through the budget and the measure, and then the room asks questions. These are the district's meetings, so there is no campaigning in them. That is also why a neighbor who hasn't decided will come if you ask. So ask.

Who's telling this story?

You do not have to explain school finance. The post that reaches a neighbor is the one only you could have written. So start with who you are. We'll offer a first line.

Add one sourced fact (optional)

One number holds a story down. Two make it a lecture. Tap add and it goes in under your words. Tap the source if you want to check it yourself.

A few things that help
  • Write the way you talk. Read it out loud once before you post it.
  • Name the school. "Field Elementary" is a place. "Our schools" is not.
  • End with the ask: "I'm voting yes on the LPS mill levy override, and I hope you will too."
  • Ask before you name your kid, someone else's kid, or a teacher. Teachers: share on your own time, never from work.

Add a picture (optional)

Posts with a picture get seen more. A picture only you could have taken gets seen most. Use your own photo, or put your line on a card. Then make it look like yours.

The pickup line. The science fair. The kid in the jersey. The photo stays on your device. The card is drawn right here in your browser and nothing is uploaded. Ask before you post someone else's kid.

When someone pushes back

  • Answer once, kindly, with a source. Then stop. A long argument is what pushes the thread into more feeds.
  • Never speak for the campaign. "As a parent, here's my understanding…" and link to citizensforlps.org.
  • If something false is spreading, don't fight it in the comments. Screenshot it and send it to the team. We answer it once, and well.
  • Neighbors who disagree are still neighbors. We are asking them for something. We can be kind about it.
