November 3, 2026 · Littleton Public Schools

Local funding that stays in LPS classrooms.

Littleton Public Schools faces a $10.6 million budget gap. The district has already cut $6.5 million, frozen wages, and scheduled an unpaid furlough day. The mill levy override on this November's ballot restores what one-time cuts can't: competitive teacher pay, full instructional days, and the programs that make LPS a destination district.

The Facts

The gap: $10.6 million, and it's structural.

Four forces created it — none of them one-time events:

    The district cut first.

    Before asking voters for anything, LPS balanced the 2026–27 budget with measures that fall on staff and students:

      Those measures work exactly once. Without ongoing local funding, they become permanent — and deepen.

      The ask: a $10 million mill levy override.

      If voters approve, educator compensation is restored retroactively to the start of the contract year and the spring furlough day comes off the calendar — students get that day of instruction back.

      Every dollar is local by law.

      A mill levy override is collected by the county and goes directly to Littleton Public Schools. The state legislature cannot redirect it, and it cannot be spent outside this district. That's the difference between an MLO and hoping the state funding formula improves.

      Key Dates

      Colorado votes by mail. Every registered voter in the LPS boundary — Littleton, parts of Centennial, and unincorporated Arapahoe County — gets a ballot automatically in mid-October. Most ballots come back in the first two weeks, so the campaign isn't one election day: it's a three-week return window.

      Not sure you're registered at your current address? Check at GoVoteColorado.gov — it takes under a minute.
