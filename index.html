This November, voters across the Littleton Public Schools community will decide a $10 million mill levy override. The campaign's home is citizensforlps.org. That's where to sign up to volunteer (the form includes "I will take a yard sign" — signs go out starting September 12) and see the event calendar. This site does one narrower thing: it helps you tell your own story about these schools, in your own words, to the people who know you.

One number anchors a story; two turn it into a lecture. Tap add and it drops in below your words — tap the source to check it yourself.

A few things that help
  • Write the way you talk. Read it out loud once before you post it.
  • Name the school. "Field Elementary" lands; "our schools" floats away.
  • End with the ask: "I'm voting yes on the LPS mill levy override, and I hope you will too."
  • Ask before you name your kid, someone else's kid, or a teacher. Teachers: share on your own time, never from work.

Give it something to see (optional)

Posts with a picture travel further, and a picture only you could have posted travels furthest. Use your own photo, or put your line on a card — then make it look like yours.

When someone pushes back

  • Answer once, kindly, with a source. Then step away — long arguments push the thread to more feeds.
  • Never speak for the campaign. "As a parent, here's my understanding…" and link to citizensforlps.org.
  • If you see something false spreading, don't fight it in the comments — screenshot it and send it to the team so we can answer it well, once.
  • Neighbors who disagree are still neighbors. We're asking them for something; we can be gracious about it.
