## 1. Issue Committee Registration and Reporting (FCPA, CRS Title 1, Article 45)

### What Citizens for LPS is, legally
An **issue committee** is any group of two or more persons with (a) a major purpose of supporting or opposing a ballot issue/question and (b) contributions or expenditures **in excess of $200** (Colo. Const. Art. XXVIII § 2(10); CRS 1-45-103). A committee supporting an LPS MLO is unambiguously an issue committee.

### Registration (verified — SOS Campaign & Political Finance Manual)
- **Register within 10 calendar days** of accepting or making contributions/expenditures exceeding **$200** (CRS 1-45-108(3)).
- A **school-district-level committee registers with the Secretary of State** and files in **TRACER** (tracer.sos.colorado.gov) — *not* with a municipal clerk. Registration requires a **registered agent** (a natural person).
- **Small-Scale Issue Committee (SSIC) tier:** if the committee will stay at or under **$5,000** in contributions *and* expenditures for the cycle, it registers as an SSIC and files no disclosure reports while between $200 and $5,000 (CRS 1-45-103(16.3)). For a $10M MLO campaign you will almost certainly exceed $5,000 — the SOS manual explicitly advises **registering directly as a full issue committee**.
- If registered as an SSIC and you cross $5,000: **notify the filing officer within 15 days**, file an initial report within 5 days of that notice, and the committee converts (CRS 1-45-108(1.5)).

### Contributions
- **No contribution limits** for issue committees — individuals, businesses, corporations, and unions may give unlimited amounts.
- **Prohibited:** anonymous contributions of $20 or more; contributions from foreign citizens, governments, or corporations; reimbursed ("straw") contributions (CRS 1-45-105.5, 1-45-108).
- **Bank account:** deposit all contributions into a **separate account titled in the committee's name** (Art. XXVIII § 3(9); CRS 1-45-107.5(7)).

### Reporting schedule for a committee on the November 2026 ballot (CRS 1-45-108(2)(a); Rule 17, 8 CCR 1505-6)
- **Biweekly reports beginning the first Monday in September** and every two weeks thereafter through the election, then a **post-election report ~35 days after**. For 2026 the approximate due dates: **Sep 8, Sep 21, Oct 5, Oct 19, Nov 2, and Dec 8, 2026** — **NEEDS VERIFICATION**: pull the exact assigned calendar from TRACER once registered.
- **Major Contribution Reports (24-hour rule):** any contribution of **$1,000 or more received within 30 days of the election (Oct 4 – Nov 3, 2026)** must be reported in TRACER **within 24 hours** (CRS 1-45-108(2.5)).

### Itemization and records (verified — SOS manual)
- Itemize **name and address** for every contributor of **$20 or more**.
- Add **occupation and employer** for natural persons giving **$100 or more**.
- **Keep all financial records for at least 180 days** following the general election; practically, until the committee terminates and any complaint window is past.
- **Late filing penalty: $50 per day**, including weekends and holidays (Art. XXVIII § 10).
- **Termination:** file a termination report at zero balance; leftover funds may be returned to contributors or donated to an IRS-recognized charity (CRS 1-45-106(3)).

## 2. "Paid for by" Disclaimers (CRS 1-45-108.3; Rule 22, 8 CCR 1505-6-22)

### Trigger (verified)
Any issue committee **or other person** spending **more than $1,000 per calendar year** on communications supporting or opposing a ballot issue — broadcast, printed, mailed, placed on a website/streaming service/online forum **for a fee**, or otherwise distributed — must include a disclaimer (CRS 1-45-108.3). Assume it applies and put the disclaimer on everything from day one.

### Required content (verified — exact wording from the SOS manual)
- **"Paid for by Citizens for LPS."**
- Because the payer is not a natural person, also **identify the registered agent by name**: **"Paid for by Citizens for LPS, [Registered Agent Name], Registered Agent."** ⚠️ ACTION: add the registered agent's name to the site footer and ad templates once confirmed.

### Format rules (verified — Rule 22, 8 CCR 1505-6-22)
- **Print/nonbroadcast (mailers, yard signs, flyers):** clear, conspicuous; font at least **15% of the largest font used**, or **at least 8-point**.
- **Broadcast:** FCC sponsorship ID applies independently.
- **Online/social/digital:** where size/format makes an on-communication disclaimer impracticable (small display ad, character-limited format), a **direct link from the ad to a page with the full statement** suffices — the link must appear on the initial communication. Website and paid social ads should carry the disclaimer directly.
- **Exempt small items:** bumper stickers, pins, buttons, pens.
- **Organic (unpaid) volunteer posts on their own accounts** are not "placed for a fee" — but committee accounts should carry the committee identity everywhere as best practice.

### Electioneering communications and independent expenditures — mostly N/A
- Colorado's "electioneering communication" and "independent expenditure" regimes are **candidate-centered**. A pure ballot-issue campaign that never references candidates does not trigger them. **Do not feature or reference school-board or other candidates in ads.**
- Spending by *other* persons over $1,000/yr on ballot-issue communications requires the spender's own disclaimer — relevant if a supportive business runs its own ads.

## 3. The Bright Line: District Communications vs. Campaign Advocacy (CRS 1-45-117)

**This section matters most given the repo owner's dual role (campaign social lead + LRPC member).**

### What the district (and anyone acting for it) may NOT do (verified)
- No agency or board of any political subdivision — **including LPS and its Board of Education** — may **make any contribution** to an issue campaign or **expend public moneys or resources to urge electors to vote for or against** a ballot issue (CRS 1-45-117(1)(a)).
- "Public resources" is read broadly: staff time on the clock, district email systems and mailing lists, copiers, the district website and official social accounts, school newsletters, robocall platforms, free facility use when others pay, and **backpack/flyer distribution channels** — none may carry *advocacy*.

### The exceptions (verified)
1. **Factual summary:** the district may spend public funds to distribute a "factual summary" that "shall include arguments both for and against" and "shall not contain a conclusion or opinion" (CRS 1-45-117(1)(b)(III)). Case law (e.g., *Colorado Common Cause v. Coffman*; the 2025 Academy District 20 SOS matter) shows one-sided "informational" pieces get districts in trouble — balance is the test.
2. **Board resolution:** the Board may pass a resolution taking a position and report it through established, customary channels — but may not spend public funds on paid advertising of it.
3. **Unsolicited-inquiry / $50 rule:** a policy-making member/employee may spend up to $50 of public funds responding to unsolicited inquiries (CRS 1-45-117(1)(b)(II)).
4. **Personal capacity — fully protected:** any board member, employee, or official "may use personal funds and time to advocate for or against ballot issues without restriction" (CRS 1-45-117(1)(b)(I)).

### Practical do/don't table

| Person | May do | May not do |
|---|---|---|
| **District employees (teachers, staff)** | Volunteer, donate, canvass, post, speak — on personal time, personal devices/accounts | Campaign during paid work time; use district email, copiers, classrooms, or student contact time for advocacy; send advocacy to parent lists |
| **Board members** | Advocate personally; vote for a board resolution; state the board's position via customary channels | Direct staff to campaign; spend district funds on advocacy/paid ads |
| **The district itself** | Distribute a balanced factual summary; answer unsolicited questions; report a board resolution through normal channels | "Vote Yes" messaging in newsletters, backpack flyers, websites, robocalls, or paid media; give the committee free use of resources others must pay for |
| **Campaign (Citizens for LPS)** | Buy district facility rentals / data on the **same terms as any member of the public** (CORA requests, standard rental rates) | Receive district email lists, staff time, printing, or distribution channels not equally available to opponents — an illegal in-kind contribution of public funds |

**Backpack flyers and school newsletters:** campaign advocacy may not go home in backpacks, district e-newsletters, or flyer platforms as a district-provided service. Safest rule: **the campaign never touches district distribution channels.**

### Wearing both hats: campaign social-media lead + Long-Range Planning Committee member
1. **Legal baseline:** serving on both is **not prohibited**. CRS 1-45-117(1)(b)(I) protects personal-capacity advocacy. The risk is *conflation*, not the dual role itself.
2. **Never advocate inside LRPC work.** No MLO promotion in LRPC meetings, agendas, or committee communications; LRPC output touching the MLO stays in the factual-summary lane.
3. **No district resources for campaign work** — ever: no district email, no non-public documents/data obtained through LRPC access, no LRPC contact lists. If campaign materials need district data, use **published/public sources or CORA** so the paper trail shows equal access.
4. **Title usage:** in campaign materials and bios, either omit the LRPC role or mark it **"for identification purposes only"** — never imply the LRPC, the Board, or LPS endorses the campaign. On personal accounts used for campaign posting, a "views my own" line is cheap insurance.
5. **Non-public information:** anything learned in LRPC that isn't public doesn't seed campaign messaging until the district publishes it.
6. **Complaint exposure:** anyone may file an FCPA complaint (CRS 1-45-111.7); MLO opponents commonly file against districts and dual-role volunteers. Clean separation — separate email, separate hours, documented public sources — is the defense. Consider disclosing the dual role to both the LRPC chair and the committee's registered agent.
7. **Check the district's own policy** on political activity (often BEDF/GBEBC-family board policies). **NEEDS VERIFICATION** — check the LPS board policy manual.

## 4. TABOR Ballot Issue Notice — a Real Campaign Task with a Hard Deadline

Under **Colo. Const. Art. X, § 20(3)(b)** (TABOR) and CRS 1-7-901 et seq.:

- **At least 30 days before the election**, a notice package addressed to "All Registered Voters" is mailed to each household with an active registered voter (the county clerk mails one combined package).
- For a tax-increase measure like an MLO, the notice includes the ballot title/text, fiscal-year spending figures, the estimated first-full-fiscal-year dollar increase — and **two summaries, up to 500 words each, one FOR and one AGAINST**, summarizing **written comments filed with the election officer by 45 days before the election**.
- **Comment rules (verified):** summaries may **not name persons or private groups, nor endorsements** — argument only. If multiple voters file on the same side, the designated election official (DEO) consolidates into one ≤500-word summary — **coordinate so supporters file one tight, agreed statement**. File with the **LPS DEO** (confirm who this is with the district office). Hand-deliver and get a time-stamped receipt.
- **The dates for November 3, 2026:**
  - 45 days before = **Saturday, September 19, 2026**. The operative deadline is commonly **noon on the preceding Friday, September 18, 2026**. **NEEDS VERIFICATION — confirm exact date/time and filing location with the LPS DEO and Arapahoe County Elections immediately.**
  - District delivers full notice content to the county clerk no later than 43 days before = ~**September 21, 2026** (CRS 1-7-904) — meaning comments must already be in.
  - Notice mailed to voters on or before ~**October 2–4, 2026**.
- **This is the single most durable piece of campaign real estate: it lands in every voter household, for free.** Draft the strongest possible 500-word FOR statement regardless of whether opponents file.

## 5. Mill Levy Override Law and the Tax Calculator (CRS 22-54-108)

### What an MLO is (verified)
- CRS 22-54-108 lets a district, **with voter approval**, levy additional local property taxes above its Total Program funding. The statute does not restrict use — MLO dollars are general operating money unless the **ballot language itself earmarks them** (and once earmarked, the district is bound; district MLO measures must also address charter-school distribution, CRS 22-32-108.5).
- **Statutory cap (verified):** total override revenue may not exceed **25% of the district's total program** (or $200K, whichever is greater; 30% for small rural districts), plus grandfathered amounts, with a transition formula through 2030. **NEEDS VERIFICATION:** LPS's remaining capacity under the cap (cite CDE's annual MLO revenue report).
- **MLO vs. bond:** MLO = ongoing **operating** revenue (people and programs), no debt. Bond = borrowed **capital** money (buildings) repaid by a separate debt-service levy. Bonds can't pay teachers.

### The 2026 tax math (verified rates; verify final mills against certified ballot language)
Post-Gallagher legislation (SB24-233 as amended by HB24B-1001) split assessment rates. For **property tax year 2026**:

- **School district levies: residential assessed at 7.05% of actual value** (subject to a possible adjustment to 6.95% only if statewide value growth exceeded 5%). **NEEDS VERIFICATION** against https://dpt.colorado.gov/residential-school-assessment-rate before publishing calculator results as final.
- Non-school local governments use a different (6.8%-family) rate — don't mix them.

**Formula:**
```
Annual MLO cost = Home actual value × 7.05% × (MLO mills ÷ 1000)
```

- LPS's published estimate for the $10M MLO: **~$25/year per $100,000 of home value** (Littleton Independent) — implies roughly **3.5 mills**. MLOs are typically certified as a fixed **dollar** amount with a floating mill rate — **NEEDS VERIFICATION** against the certified ballot language.
- **Worked examples at ~$25 per $100K:** median Littleton home ≈ $625K–$677K → ≈ **$156–$169/year (~$13–14/month)**. Arapahoe County typical value ≈ $516K–$529K → ≈ **$129–$132/year**.
- Calculator honesty rules: label the assessment rate and tax year; note commercial property pays at ~27–29% assessment; note the mill rate floats if the MLO is a fixed-dollar authorization; cite sources on the page.

## 6. Platform Rules for Paid Ads

### Meta (Facebook/Instagram)
- Ads about "social issues, elections or politics" — **explicitly including ballot measures** — require: (1) **identity verification** (government ID + postal mail code to a US residential address, typically 5–10 business days), (2) a **"Paid for by" disclaimer** matching the committee's registered name, (3) a linked Page with authorized admin. Ads enter the public **Ad Library for 7 years**.
- **Lead time: start 3–4 weeks before the first ad.** For ads live by early October, **start in early September 2026 at the latest** — ideally now. Verify at least two admins; never disable 2FA.

### Google Ads / YouTube
- US Election Ads verification covers candidates and **ballot measures**; whether a district-level measure is technically in scope is ambiguous — verify anyway (**~5–7 business days**). The **end advertiser (the committee), not an agency,** must verify.

### Nextdoor
- **No self-serve political ads** — ads advocating for/against a ballot proposition are prohibited. Plan on zero paid Nextdoor presence.
- **Organic activity is allowed**: real neighbors posting as themselves may discuss local ballot measures civilly and post local political events. No fake accounts; expect posts perceived as campaign spam to be removed.
