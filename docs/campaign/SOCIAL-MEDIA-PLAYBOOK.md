**Prepared for the Citizens for LPS Social Media team lead.** Citizens for LPS is a registered Colorado issue committee (co-chairs Amy Clark and Briana McCrumb) supporting the $10M MLO the LPS board referred to the November 2026 ballot on a 5-0 vote ([citizensforlps.org](https://citizensforlps.org/)). This playbook covers platform strategy, content, volunteer amplification, opposition response, paid social, legal disclosure, accessibility, and a week-by-week arc.

---

## 0. Ground truth: the dates everything hangs on

| Date | What happens | Social implication |
|---|---|---|
| **Sept 1 – early Oct** | Persuasion window | Story-building, myth-busting, endorsements |
| **~Oct 12–16** | Colorado statutory ballot-mailing window (18–22 days before Election Day); most voters have ballots in hand by ~Oct 19. **Confirm the exact Arapahoe County mail date at ArapahoeVotes.gov** | **"Ballot box" moment** — pivot from persuasion to "ballots are here, vote now" |
| **Oct 20 (approx.)** | Last recommended day to return ballots by USPS | Shift all CTAs to **drop boxes** |
| **Oct 26** | Last day to register and still receive a mail ballot | Registration push ends |
| **~Oct 27 – Nov 3** | **Meta blackout: no NEW political/social-issue ads in the final week** — only ads that already served ≥1 impression may keep running | **Every ad creative you want live in the final week must be launched and serving by ~Oct 26** |
| **Nov 3, 7 p.m.** | Ballots must be *received* (postmarks don't count) | GOTV chase peaks |

**Critical structural fact for Colorado:** in an all-mail state, "Election Day" is really a **three-week voting window starting mid-October**. Your persuasion campaign must be essentially complete by ballot drop — many of your voters will vote the week ballots arrive.

**Critical timeline flag (start this week):** Meta political-ad authorization takes days to weeks — identity verification, disclaimer approval, page linking — and reviewers slow down in election season. Guides consistently say start **30+ days before your first ad flight**. If it's not done, it is the single most urgent item in this document.

## 1. Platform strategy: where to spend effort (and where not to)

### Facebook — the primary battlefield (≈60% of effort)
Still dominant for your three highest-value audiences: parents 35–55, seniors, and hyperlocal community conversation. School-measure campaigns that win do it largely on Facebook.

- **Official page** (Citizens for LPS): the canonical source. 3–5 posts/week. All paid media runs from here.
- **Local groups are where the votes are**: Littleton/Centennial community groups, school-specific parent groups (PTO pages), neighborhood buy-nothing/moms groups. The official page usually *can't* post there — **members can**. Your volunteer network (Section 3) is the delivery mechanism. Elk River's winning bond campaign explicitly had supporters embedded in private Facebook groups monitoring and responding.
- **Events**: create FB Events for every coffee chat, info night, and ballot-drop party — events get organic distribution posts don't.
- **Live video**: a monthly "Ask Us Anything" livestream with a co-chair + a teacher (on personal time — see Section 6) performs well with seniors.

### Instagram — the parent/young-voter secondary (≈20% of effort)
Same Meta ad system, same content adapted. Reels (vertical, <60s, captioned) and Stories with the link sticker to citizensforlps.org. Carousels for myth-vs-fact and tax-math content. Use the collaborative-post feature so endorsing organizations' posts co-appear on your grid. Instagram "Add Yours" story stickers ("Why I'm voting yes for LPS") are the modern profile-frame analog.

### Nextdoor — high value, strict rules (≈10% of effort, all organic, all volunteer)
Nextdoor is uniquely valuable for seniors and non-parent homeowners — exactly your swing audience — but it has the most specific political rules of any platform:

- **Local political topics ARE allowed in the main feed** — a local ballot initiative is explicitly the example of permitted "local" content. National politics is restricted to groups; your MLO is squarely in-bounds.
- **But ongoing campaigning is not**: introductions are fine; sustained campaign announcements, repeated advocacy, and fundraising must live in a **dedicated Nextdoor Group** (create "Support Littleton Public Schools — 2026 MLO"). **Over-posting about your issue is a violation** and Nextdoor "should not be used as a campaign mailing list."
- **No political ads**: Nextdoor's self-serve ads prohibit ads that advocate for/against a ballot proposition. Zero paid budget here.
- **How campaigns actually navigate it**: recruit one or two authentic residents per Nextdoor neighborhood who post *personally and occasionally* ("As a 30-year Littleton resident, here's why I'm voting yes…"), answer questions civilly, and link to the FAQ page. One thoughtful post per neighborhood per phase (September intro → ballot-drop reminder → final-week drop-box post) stays inside the rules; a daily barrage gets posts removed and goodwill burned. Never argue in Nextdoor threads — answer once, link to the FAQ, exit.

### YouTube — the video library (≈5% of effort)
Host all testimonial and explainer videos here; embed on citizensforlps.org; cut vertical versions for Reels/TikTok. If you ever run YouTube ads, complete Google's election-ads verification anyway. For a district-sized campaign, YouTube paid is optional; organic hosting is the point.

### TikTok — organic-only, low priority (≈5%, only if you have a natural creator)
TikTok **bans all paid political advertising, including paying creators and using promotional tools to boost political videos** — but fully allows organic political content; political accounts also can't include donation links. Only invest if a volunteer genuinely enjoys making short vertical video — "what a furlough day actually means," "the tax math in 20 seconds." Never boost; never pay a creator.

### X/Twitter — press-release channel only (≈0–2%)
Local voters aren't there in numbers. Maintain a handle to reach local reporters (Littleton Independent, Colorado Community Media, Denver TV education reporters) and mirror major announcements. Don't build strategy here.

**Where NOT to spend effort:** Nextdoor ads (prohibited), TikTok ads (prohibited), X ads, Snapchat, LinkedIn, Threads, and any platform where you'd be building an audience from zero in a 10-week window.

## 2. Content strategy: what actually moves local school-funding voters

### The message discipline
Every post maps to one of three anchor frames (and the winnable-middle logic — roughly 10% will never vote yes, 10% already will; you're talking to the 80% in the middle):

1. **Local money, local control.** "Every dollar stays in LPS classrooms. Not one cent goes to the state." (This is the frame for conservative-leaning and senior voters — *this is your community choosing to fund its own schools*.)
2. **Keep our teachers.** LPS already imposed a wage freeze and a furlough day; negotiated compensation is contingent on the MLO passing.
3. **The math is small and honest.** ~$25/year per $100,000 of home value (Littleton Independent). "Less than $15/month for most homes" beats abstractions; "the cost of two coffees a month" makes it shareable.

### Content types, ranked by what moves local voters

1. **Testimonial video (the workhorse).** <60 seconds, vertical, captioned, one person, one point. Rotate the cast deliberately: a veteran teacher, a parent, a **senior/grandparent** ("strong schools are why my home is worth what it is"), a **self-identified fiscal conservative** ("I read the budget; this is the responsible option"), a small-business owner, and students *only with signed parent/guardian consent and never implying district endorsement*.
2. **Myth-vs-Fact cards.** A branded recurring series (same template every time, so shares are instantly recognizable). Pre-bunk the predictable attacks (Section 4). When a rumor recurs, it goes on the website FAQ *and* becomes a graphic with talking points for supporters.
3. **The tax math / calculator share.** "What will it cost MY house?" is the #1 question. A graphic ladder ($400K home → $X/yr; $600K → $Y/yr; $800K → $Z/yr) plus a calculator on the website.
4. **"Day in the life" classroom-adjacent content.** Positive, warm school-pride content builds the trust that campaign asks spend. Source from parents/volunteers with school permission, not district staff on duty (Section 6).
5. **Endorsement graphics.** One template, filled weekly: community leaders, the teachers' association, PTOs, youth sports leagues, local businesses, retired principals, "Republicans for LPS" / "Seniors for LPS" validator sets. Endorsements from *unexpected* validators move the winnable middle most.
6. **"What happens if it fails" content — used sparingly and factually.** The district's own published cut list is your evidence. State facts, not threats; fear-based messaging backfires with skeptics.
7. **Countdown/GOTV mechanics content (Phase 3).** "Ballots arrive this week," drop-box maps, ballot selfies (with the *envelope*, not the marked ballot), "I voted yes" frames, countdown graphics.

### Cadence and the three-phase arc

- **Official page cadence:** 3–5 posts/week Sept; daily from ballot drop; 2×/day final four days.
- **Phase 1 — Persuasion (Sept 1 – Oct 9):** who we are, why now, what it costs, who supports it, myth-busting. 70% story/testimonial, 20% facts/math, 10% logistics.
- **Phase 2 — Ballot drop (Oct 12 – Oct 24):** "Your ballot is here." Every post ends with a voting CTA. 40% testimonial, 20% endorsement, 40% vote-now logistics.
- **Phase 3 — Chase/GOTV (Oct 25 – Nov 3):** drop boxes only after ~Oct 20 USPS cutoff. Urgency, countdowns, "have you returned it?", relational chase. Persuasion content is over.

## 3. The volunteer amplification model: 100+ supporters sharing consistently

The algorithm reality: an official campaign page reaches a fraction of its followers; a personal post from a neighbor reaches more people *and* is more persuasive. Texts from people voters personally know raise turnout ~8.6 points vs. control.

### Structure: the Amplifier Corps
- **Recruit 100–150 "Amplifiers"** from the sign-up form on citizensforlps.org. Segment by school community and by neighborhood (for Nextdoor coverage).
- **One broadcast channel** — a WhatsApp Community or broadcast list organized as: 1 announcement channel (admins post; 2–3 messages/week max) + school-level sub-groups with captains. Every message contains: the content to share, a suggested personal framing line, and one-tap links. **The rule: personalize, don't copy-paste** — 100 identical posts read as astroturf; 100 posts that each open with one personal sentence read as a movement.
- **Weekly "Share Kit" (Sundays)**: a shared folder with the week's 3 graphics (sized per platform), 1 video, suggested captions in three voices (parent / senior / taxpayer), and the week's talking points.
- **Relational organizing app (optional but proven)**: Reach (reach.vote) gives volunteers a share library, scripts, and lets them match their own contacts to the voter file for the GOTV chase. The killer app is the **October chase**: volunteers text *their own friends* "have you returned your ballot?" — the single highest-ROI GOTV activity in a mail state.
- **Profile-photo campaign**: Facebook retired its frame tool; use Twibbonize (or similar) for a "Yes for LPS" profile-photo overlay; launch as a coordinated "frame day" the day ballots mail, plus an Instagram "Add Yours" sticker chain.

### Comment-section etiquette (train every Amplifier)
1. **Be first, be positive**: 3–5 supportive comments in the first hour of every official post teaches the algorithm to distribute it and sets thread tone.
2. **One reply rule** in hostile threads: correct the record once, politely, with a link to the FAQ — then disengage. Arguing extends the thread's reach.
3. **Never speak for the campaign** — "As a parent, my understanding is… the details are at citizensforlps.org."
4. **Flag, don't fight**: screenshots of new misinformation go to the rapid-response channel, not into a personal flame war.
5. **Like-bomb the good stuff**: reacting to and sharing each other's posts multiplies reach at zero cost.

## 4. Opposition and misinformation: the rapid-response protocol

### Pre-bunk the predictable attacks (September, before opponents define you)
Every school-tax campaign faces the same five; publish the answers *first* as your Myth-vs-Fact series:
1. *"Taxes are already too high / I can't afford it."* → The exact math ($25/yr per $100K value); senior validators; senior property-tax relief programs that apply.
2. *"The district wastes money / admin bloat."* → Per-pupil funding facts, what the state cut, and where MLO dollars are committed.
3. *"They'll just ask again." / "It never goes to classrooms."* → The specific uses: teacher compensation restoration, ending furlough days; MLO dollars are locally controlled and can't be taken by the state.
4. *"Declining enrollment, why more money?"* → Costs that don't scale with enrollment; what's already been cut.
5. **Culture-war imports** (Elk River fielded CRT rumors and traced ~half of its 34 misinformation "high flyers" to out-of-state accounts). → Don't amplify; redirect to the local, financial question on the ballot.

### The protocol
- **Monitor**: 2–3 volunteers own daily sweeps of the big local Facebook groups, Nextdoor, and post comments; a shared rapid-response channel receives screenshots.
- **Triage — the 3-question engage/ignore test**: (1) Is it *spreading*? (2) Is it *new* (not already answered on the FAQ)? (3) Is the venue *winnable* (a community group, not the opposition's own page)? Three yeses → respond within 24h with a calm, factual, sourced correction from a *volunteer voice*, plus add it to the website FAQ and, if it keeps spreading, a Myth-vs-Fact graphic. Fewer than three → log it and ignore. **Never respond on the opposition's own page, never quote-share their content**, and never punch down at an individual resident.
- **Escalation**: genuinely defamatory content → co-chairs decide on an official statement; coordinated inauthentic activity → platform reporting.

### Comment moderation policy for official pages (publish it in the About section)
Post a visible, viewpoint-neutral policy: *"We welcome questions and disagreement. We remove: profanity, personal attacks, spam, misinformation stated as fact, and off-topic content. Repeat violations = ban."* Then apply it evenly — deleting mere disagreement becomes its own story. Use keyword filters to auto-hide. **Hide rather than delete** where possible. Answer every good-faith question within 24h, publicly.

## 5. Paid social for a district-sized campaign

### Meta authorization — START NOW (Week 1 task)
1. Whoever will run ads verifies **personal identity**: US government ID + US residential mailing address; Meta may mail a confirmation code. Days to weeks.
2. A **Page admin** creates the **"Paid for by Citizens for LPS" disclaimer**, verified via EIN, website+email, or address method; review typically 24–48h but can stretch.
3. Link the ad account to the Page; enable the ads-about-social-issues toggle. **Never disable two-factor authentication** — it voids the authorization and you restart from zero.
4. Verify **at least two admins** so one person's vacation doesn't dark your ads.
- All political ads are archived publicly in the **Ad Library for 7 years** — assume every ad will be screenshotted by opponents; Meta requires **disclosure of AI-generated/altered ad content**.

### Geo-targeting rules (special ad category)
Ads about social issues/elections run under restricted targeting: limited demographic targeting, but city/radius targeting works. Practical setup for LPS: target **Littleton + Centennial by city/radius pins approximating district boundaries**, age 25+. Accept ~20–30% spill outside district lines. You **can** retarget people who engaged with your Page/video — your best audience.

### Budget: what a district-sized campaign should plan
Benchmarks: Meta CPMs of roughly **$5–15** for local political content. The LPS electorate is on the order of ~60–80K households. A workable plan:

| Phase | Budget | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Sept (persuasion) | $1,500–3,000 | Video views + page growth: testimonial videos to the full geo |
| Oct 1–24 (ballot drop) | $3,000–5,000 | Frequency on the 2–3 best creatives + endorsements + "ballots are here" |
| Oct 25–Nov 3 (chase) | $1,500–2,500 | Drop-box/GOTV creative — **already serving before the blackout** |
| **Total** | **$6K–10K** | ~8–15 impressions per district household over 9 weeks |

If the committee can only fund $3K total, spend it Oct 5–26 on the two best testimonial videos + the tax-math graphic.

### Boosted posts vs. Ads Manager
- **Boosting** still requires the full political authorization + disclaimer — no disclosure shortcut.
- **Ads Manager** gets you placement control, A/B tests, video-view retargeting, lifetime budgets with scheduled flights, and frequency caps. Boost occasionally to reward winners; plan the real flights in Ads Manager.
- **Blackout choreography**: every final-week ad must have served ≥1 impression before the restriction (~Oct 27) begins. Launch all GOTV creatives at low spend by **Oct 24**, then scale spend during the blackout (raising budget on a running ad is allowed; new creative is not).

## 6. Legal and disclosure requirements (Colorado + platform)

**This is guidance, not legal advice — have the registered agent confirm with the committee's counsel and the Colorado SOS.** See also `docs/research/colorado-compliance.md`.

### Colorado "paid for by" rules
- Under **C.R.S. 1-45-108.3**, an issue committee spending **more than $1,000/calendar year** on covered communications must include a disclaimer; covered communications include those placed on a website or online forum **for a fee**. Paid social ads are squarely covered; the SOS recommends disclaimers on *all* communications, especially anything that may be re-shared.
- **Practical rule: bake "Paid for by Citizens for LPS" into every graphic and video end-card**, put it in the Page bio/About on every platform, and on the website footer.
- Route **in-kind services** (donated design, ad spend by supporters) to the registered agent for TRACER reporting.

### The FCPA firewall (protects your teachers and the district)
Colorado's Fair Campaign Practices Act bars the **district** from spending public money urging a yes vote; district staff **may advocate on personal time with personal resources**, may not campaign **on duty**, and may not use district email/website/social accounts for advocacy; the district may only distribute a balanced factual summary. Operational consequences:
- Teacher testimonials: filmed **off school grounds or outside contract hours**, framed as personal opinion, never implying district production. No district logos as if official.
- Never ask LPS staff to share committee content from school accounts or during the workday; give teachers a one-page "what you can/can't do" card — it protects them and prevents an FCPA complaint becoming the October news cycle.
- The district's own factual budget pages are safe to *link*; the advocacy layer is yours alone.

### Consent
Signed media releases for every identifiable adult; **parent/guardian signed consent for any minor**, stored by the committee. Students never filmed at school for committee content.

## 7. Accessibility and trust practices

These aren't polish — seniors are a core audience, and accessible content is also the most-shared content.

- **Alt text on every image**: describe the content and any text in the graphic; don't repeat the caption.
- **Captions on every video, always** — most social video plays muted; hand-correct auto-captions (proper nouns, dollar figures). Burned-in captions for Reels/TikTok.
- **CamelCase every hashtag**: #YesOnLPS, #LittletonPublicSchools — screen readers can't parse #yesonlps.
- **Text-in-graphic rules**: minimum ~24px equivalent, WCAG AA contrast (4.5:1), never put the tax math *only* in an image — repeat key numbers in the post text.
- **No emoji-as-bullets, no fancy Unicode fonts**; emoji at the end of sentences, sparingly.
- **One canonical home**: every post, every ad, every volunteer share links to **citizensforlps.org**. A single authoritative hub is the most consistent trait of winning campaigns.
- **Consistent branding**: one logo, one palette, one type treatment, templated for the whole volunteer corps; the disclaimer built into the templates. Recognizability is what makes the 40th impression land.

## 9. Week-by-week content arc: Sept 1 – Nov 3, 2026

**Standing weekly rhythm (all phases):** Mon = facts/math · Wed = testimonial video · Fri = endorsement or community post · Sun = Share Kit to Amplifiers + week-ahead schedule. Rapid-response and comment coverage run daily throughout.

### PHASE 1 — PERSUASION (Weeks 1–6)

**Week 1 (Sept 1–7) — Infrastructure week.**
- START Meta political-ads authorization (two admins). Audit/claim handles on FB, IG, YouTube, X; create the Nextdoor group; add social links + "Paid for by Citizens for LPS" + FAQ + tax table to citizensforlps.org.
- Launch post across platforms: "Who we are and why we're doing this" (co-chairs, 45-sec video). Publish the comment moderation policy.
- Begin Amplifier recruitment from the website sign-up list; stand up the WhatsApp tree; build the template kit.

**Week 2 (Sept 8–14) — Frame the problem.**
- The story of the $10.6M cut, the furlough day, and the wage freeze — told by a parent, not a spreadsheet. Link to district budget page for the facts layer.
- First Myth-vs-Fact card: "Where does the money actually go?" First Nextdoor volunteer intro posts (one per neighborhood, personal voice).
- Film day #1: batch-record 6–8 testimonials (teachers off-duty, parents, a senior, a conservative validator).

**Week 3 (Sept 15–21) — The math week.**
- Tax-impact ladder graphic + calculator page push: "$25/year per $100K of home value." Repeat the numbers in plain text for accessibility.
- Testimonial #2: veteran teacher. Myth-vs-Fact #2 (pre-bunk "admin bloat").
- First paid flight (if authorization cleared): $50–75/day video views, district geo.

**Week 4 (Sept 22–28) — Local money, local control.**
- "Every dollar stays local" explainer (30-sec animation or carousel). Testimonial #3: senior/grandparent on home values and community.
- First endorsement graphics batch. FB Live #1: co-chair + retired principal Q&A.
- Amplifier "frame day" prep: build the profile-photo overlay.

**Week 5 (Sept 29–Oct 5) — Validators week.**
- Fiscal-conservative testimonial — aimed at the winnable middle. Endorsement roll: teachers' association, PTOs, businesses.
- Myth-vs-Fact #3 ("they'll just ask again" / enrollment). Monitor DPS/DougCo MLO feeds for attack lines migrating your way; pre-bunk anything spreading.
- Verify all GOTV creative concepts are drafted (drop-box map, countdown set, "ballots are here" video scripts).

**Week 6 (Oct 6–12) — Pivot staging.**
- "Your ballot is coming" explainer: what the measure line will look like on the ballot, how mail voting works, drop-box locations (link ArapahoeVotes.gov / GoVoteColorado.gov).
- Last major persuasion pushes: best-performing testimonial re-boosted; FB Live #2 "Ask Us Anything before you vote."
- Profile-frame day launch. Registration-deadline reminders begin (online/mail by Oct 26).

### PHASE 2 — BALLOT DROP (Weeks 7–8)

**Week 7 (Oct 13–19) — "It's here."** *(Ballots hit mailboxes this week — confirm exact Arapahoe date.)*
- Daily posting begins. Hero post: "Check your mailbox — your ballot is here. Find [the LPS measure] and vote YES." Ballot-envelope selfie chain via Amplifiers (#YesOnLPS, envelope not marked ballot).
- Testimonials re-cut with new end-card: "Ballots are out. Vote yes today." Paid spend shifts to vote-now creative + retargeting video viewers.
- Amplifier relational ask #1: text five friends "did your ballot arrive?"
- Nextdoor round 2: one "ballots are here, happy to answer questions" post per neighborhood.

**Week 8 (Oct 20–26) — Bank the votes.**
- **Oct 20: USPS cutoff messaging flips — "From today, use a drop box."** Drop-box map graphic becomes the most-repeated asset.
- "Why I already voted yes" UGC wave. Endorsement recap carousel. Oct 26 registration deadline final call.
- **CRITICAL: by Oct 24–26, launch every final-week ad at low budget so each records impressions before Meta's new-ad blackout (~Oct 27).**
- Amplifier relational ask #2: the chase begins — "have you returned it?"

### PHASE 3 — CHASE / GOTV (Week 9 + Election Day)

**Week 9 (Oct 27–Nov 2) — All chase, no persuasion.**
- 2 posts/day: countdown graphics ("7 days — ballots must be RECEIVED by 7pm Nov 3"), drop-box map, 15-sec urgency clips from your best messengers. Scale budgets on already-running ads.
- Amplifiers text every unreturned contact; broadcast tree goes daily. Stories takeovers: "drive with me to the drop box."
- Rapid-response on high alert — last-minute attacks are the reason Meta's blackout exists; keep response graphics templated and ready.

**Election Day (Tue Nov 3).**
- Morning: "TODAY. Drop box by 7 p.m. — mail is too late." Repost at noon and 4 p.m. with nearest-box finder. Stories every 2 hours.
- 7 p.m.: pencils down. Evening: gratitude post — drafted in advance in both outcomes' versions.
- Nov 4+: results follow-up, and archive everything — the next campaign inherits this playbook.
