# Social Media Playbook — Citizens for LPS

## Yes on the LPS mill levy override · September 1 to November 3, 2026

This is how the social media team plans to spend the next nine weeks.
It comes out of the research we did in August (platform policy,
Colorado campaign law, campaigns that won) and the facts the district
has published. Where the law is involved, the registered agent has the
last word, not this document. Sources are at the bottom.

Updated September 2, 2026. The ballot-mail date is now confirmed:
Arapahoe County starts mailing ballots on **October 2**. Everything
below is built around that.

---

## 0. The dates

| Date | What happens | What we do about it |
|---|---|---|
| Sept 1 to Oct 1 | People are still deciding | Stories, the math, the answers to the questions we know are coming |
| Sept 14 to Oct 1 | The district's seven Dollars and Sense forums | Invite neighbors. Go. Listen to what people ask |
| ~Sept 18 (noon) | TABOR pro/con comments due to the district | Not a social task, but the FOR statement reaches every household free. Do not miss it |
| **Oct 2** | Arapahoe County starts mailing ballots | Everything shifts to "your ballot is here" |
| Oct 19 | County voter service and polling centers open | Point people who lost a ballot here |
| Oct 26 | Last day to register and still get a ballot by mail. Also the last day we'll tell anyone to mail a ballot | After this, drop boxes only |
| ~Oct 27 to Nov 3 | Meta will not approve new political ads in the final week | Anything we want running on Nov 1 has to be live and serving by Oct 26 |
| Nov 3, 7 p.m. | Ballots must be received. Postmarks do not count | The last push |

Colorado votes by mail. That means Election Day is a month long and
starts October 2. Most of the people we are trying to persuade will
vote the week their ballot shows up. Our persuasion work has to be
mostly done by then.

One more thing that has to start now: Meta's political-ad
authorization. Identity verification, the disclaimer, linking the
page. It takes days on a good week and longer in October. If it isn't
started, it is the most urgent item on this page.

## 1. Where we spend our time

**Facebook.** This is where the voters are. Parents 35 to 55, seniors,
and the neighborhood groups where people argue about stop signs. The
official page posts three to five times a week and is where all paid
media runs. But the page can't post in the local groups. Members can.
That is what the volunteer network is for.

Facebook Events for every coffee, info night, and ballot-drop party.
Events get distribution that posts don't. A monthly live Q&A with a
co-chair and a teacher (on the teacher's own time) reaches
grandparents.

**Instagram.** Parents and younger voters. Reels under a minute with
captions on, Stories with the link sticker to citizensforlps.org,
carousels for the tax math and the myth-vs-fact series. Collaborative
posts with endorsing organizations. An "Add Yours" story sticker:
"the LPS teacher I still think about."

**Nextdoor.** Seniors and homeowners without kids in the schools. That
is the audience we most need and least reach anywhere else. Nextdoor's
rules are specific and we follow them exactly:

- Civil discussion of a local ballot measure is allowed in the main
  feed. Our measure qualifies.
- Over-posting about it is not allowed. Nextdoor says it "should not
  be used as a campaign mailing list." Sustained campaign content
  belongs in a dedicated Nextdoor group.
- Nextdoor does not allow political ads at all. No paid budget here.

So: one or two real residents per neighborhood, posting as themselves,
once per phase. An introduction in September. "Ballots are here" in
early October. A drop-box reminder in the last week. Answer questions
kindly, link to the FAQ, and leave. Nobody argues in a Nextdoor thread.

**YouTube.** The place the videos live. Embed them on
citizensforlps.org, cut vertical versions for Reels. Organic only
unless the committee decides otherwise.

**TikTok.** Only if a volunteer already makes short video and enjoys
it. TikTok bans all paid political content, including paying creators.
Organic is allowed. "What a furlough day means at our house" in twenty
seconds could work. Nobody boosts anything.

**Threads and Bluesky.** Small local reach, near-zero effort thanks to
the studio's one-tap buttons. We do not build a presence there.

**X.** We are not on X. Reporters get an email.

## 2. What we say

We are talking to the people in the middle. About one in ten voters
will never vote yes and one in ten already will. Everything we post is
for the other eight.

Three things we say, over and over, in different voices:

1. **The money stays here.** A mill levy override is local money under
   Colorado law. Collected here, spent in LPS schools, controlled by
   the board we elect. The district's own line: "100% of the funds
   will stay local." This is the frame for skeptical and older voters.
   It is their community deciding to fund its own schools.
2. **Keep our teachers.** LPS already froze wages and put a furlough
   day on the calendar (Friday, March 19, 2027). The district says
   $2.5 million of the override is a 2% average raise and $800,000
   cancels the furlough day.
3. **The math is small and it is the district's own math.** About $25
   a year per $100,000 of home value. The district's example: a
   $600,000 home, less than $13 a month.

### What moves people, in order

1. **One person, one point, under a minute, captioned.** A veteran
   teacher. A parent. A grandparent whose home is worth what it is
   because of these schools. Someone who calls themselves a fiscal
   conservative and read the budget. A business owner. Students only
   with a parent's signed consent and never in a way that implies the
   district made it.
2. **Myth vs. fact cards.** Same template every time so a share is
   recognizable at a glance. We publish the answers before the
   questions get loud (Section 4).
3. **The tax math.** "What does it cost my house" is the first question
   everyone asks. A ladder graphic ($400K, $600K, $800K) and the
   calculator on the toolkit site, which opens on the district's own
   example.
4. **School life.** Warm, ordinary posts about what happens in these
   buildings. This is the trust the asks spend later. From parents and
   volunteers with school permission, never from staff on duty.
5. **Endorsements.** One template, filled weekly. The unexpected ones
   move the middle most.
6. **What happens if it fails.** Sparingly, and only what the district
   itself has published: the wage freeze stays, more furlough days are
   possible, career programs get capped. Facts. Not threats. Fear
   backfires with the very people we need.
7. **Ballot mechanics** once ballots are out. Drop-box maps. "Have you
   returned it?" Envelope selfies (the envelope, never the marked
   ballot).

### The rhythm

The official page: three to five posts a week in September. Daily from
October 2. Twice a day the last four days.

- **Sept 1 to Oct 1.** Who we are. Why now. What it costs. Who is with
  us. The answers to the predictable attacks. Mostly stories.
- **Oct 2 to Oct 26.** Your ballot is here. Every post ends with the
  ask to vote. Stories, endorsements, and logistics in equal parts.
- **Oct 27 to Nov 3.** Drop boxes only. Countdowns. Have you turned it
  in. Persuasion is over. This is the chase.

## 3. The volunteers

A page reaches a fraction of its followers. A neighbor's post reaches
more people and is believed more. And a text from someone you know
raises turnout more than anything else we can do. That is why the
toolkit exists.

**Recruit 100 to 150 people** from the volunteer form on
citizensforlps.org. Sort them by school and by neighborhood so every
Nextdoor neighborhood has a voice.

**One broadcast channel.** A WhatsApp community or broadcast list. Two
or three messages a week from the admins, never more, plus small
groups by school with a captain each. Every message has three parts:
the thing to share, one suggested opening line the person should
change, and the links.

**The rule is personalize, never copy-paste.** A hundred identical
posts read as a fake. A hundred posts that each open with one true
sentence read as a movement. The studio at the toolkit site is built
for exactly this: pick a voice, finish a stem, add one sourced fact if
you want one, send it where your people are.

**Sunday share kit.** The week's three graphics sized per platform, one
video, suggested captions in three voices (parent, senior, taxpayer),
and the week's talking points.

**The October chase.** Volunteers text their own friends: "did your
ballot come?" and then "have you sent it back?" Reach (reach.vote) can
match a volunteer's contacts to the voter file if the committee wants
that. Even without it, five texts per volunteer to people who live
here is the highest-value hour anyone will spend this fall.

**Profile photos.** Facebook killed its frame tool; Twibbonize or
similar for a "Yes for LPS" overlay. One coordinated day: October 2,
when ballots mail.

### In the comments

1. Be early and be kind. Three to five supportive comments in the first
   hour teach Facebook to show the post to more people.
2. Answer once. In a hostile thread, correct the record one time,
   politely, with a link to the FAQ. Then stop. Arguing is what spreads
   the thread.
3. Never speak for the campaign. "As a parent, my understanding is…
   the details are at citizensforlps.org."
4. Flag, don't fight. New misinformation gets a screenshot sent to the
   rapid-response channel, not a reply.
5. Like and share each other's posts. It costs nothing and it works.

## 4. When people push back

### Answer the five questions before anyone asks

Every school tax campaign hears the same five. We publish the answers
in September as the myth-vs-fact series.

1. *"Taxes are already too high."* The exact math. Senior validators.
   The state's senior property-tax exemption, for those who qualify.
2. *"The district wastes money."* Central administration is less than
   2% of the operating budget (Citizens for LPS analysis). What the
   state cut. Where the district says the override dollars go.
3. *"They'll just ask again." "It never reaches classrooms."* The
   district's own list: the 2% raise, the furlough day cancelled, math
   and reading staffing, career programs, counselors and safety staff.
   Local money the state cannot take.
4. *"Enrollment is falling. Why more money?"* Costs that don't fall with
   enrollment. What has already been cut ($1.1M central office, $2.8M
   operations, the wage freeze, the furlough day).
5. *Culture-war imports.* Elk River traced about half of its
   misinformation to out-of-state accounts. We don't amplify. We
   redirect to the local, financial question on the ballot.

### The protocol

- **Watch.** Two or three volunteers sweep the big local Facebook
  groups, Nextdoor, and our own comments daily. Screenshots go to one
  channel.
- **Decide.** Three questions. Is it spreading? Is it new (not already
  on the FAQ)? Is the venue one we can win (a community group, not the
  opposition's own page)? Three yeses: answer within a day, calmly,
  with a source, in a volunteer's voice, and add it to the FAQ. If it
  keeps spreading, it becomes a card. Fewer than three: log it and let
  it go.
- **Never** respond on the opposition's page. Never quote-share their
  content. Never go after an individual neighbor.
- **Escalate** anything defamatory to the co-chairs. Coordinated fake
  accounts get reported to the platform.

### Moderation on the official pages

Post the policy where people can see it: we welcome questions and
disagreement; we remove profanity, personal attacks, spam, false
claims stated as fact, and off-topic posts; repeat violations get a
ban. Then apply it the same way to everyone. Deleting disagreement
becomes its own story. Hide rather than delete when you can. Answer
every good-faith question within a day, in public.

## 5. Paid

### Meta authorization. Start this week.

1. Whoever runs the ads verifies their identity with Meta: government
   ID, US address, possibly a mailed code. Days to weeks.
2. A page admin creates the "Paid for by Citizens for LPS" disclaimer
   (EIN, website and email, or address). Usually a day or two. Sometimes
   longer.
3. Link the ad account, turn on the social-issues toggle. Never turn
   off two-factor. Doing so voids the authorization and you start over.
4. Verify two admins so one vacation doesn't take the ads down.

Every political ad sits in the public Ad Library for seven years.
Assume the opposition screenshots all of them. Meta requires disclosure
of AI-generated or altered content in ads.

### Targeting

Social-issue ads get restricted targeting, but city and radius pins
work. Pin Littleton and Centennial to approximate the district, age
25 and up, and accept some spill outside the lines. Retargeting people
who watched our videos or engaged with the page is allowed and is the
best audience we have.

### Budget

Local political CPMs run roughly $5 to $15. The district is on the
order of 60,000 to 80,000 households.

| When | Budget | For |
|---|---|---|
| September | $1,500 to $3,000 | Video views and page growth across the whole geo |
| Oct 2 to 26 | $3,000 to $5,000 | The two or three best testimonials, endorsements, "ballots are here" |
| Oct 27 to Nov 3 | $1,500 to $2,500 | Drop-box and GOTV creative, already serving before the blackout |
| Total | $6,000 to $10,000 | Roughly 8 to 15 impressions per household |

If the committee can only fund $3,000, spend it October 2 to 26 on the
two best testimonials and the tax-math graphic.

Boosting a post still requires the full political authorization. Ads
Manager gives you scheduling, A/B tests, retargeting, and frequency
caps. Plan the real flights there. Launch every final-week ad at a low
budget by October 24 so each one records an impression before the
blackout. Raising the budget on a running ad is allowed during the
blackout. New creative is not.

## 6. The law

This is guidance, not legal advice. The registered agent confirms all
of it with counsel and the Secretary of State. The long version is in
`docs/research/colorado-compliance.md`.

**"Paid for by."** Under C.R.S. 1-45-108.3, an issue committee that
spends more than $1,000 a year on covered communications needs a
disclaimer, and paid placement online is covered. The SOS recommends
the disclaimer on everything, especially anything that gets re-shared.
So it goes into every graphic, every video end card, every page bio,
and the committee site's footer. Donated design or ad spend goes to
the registered agent for TRACER reporting.

**The firewall between the district and the campaign.** Colorado's
Fair Campaign Practices Act bars the district from spending public
money to urge a yes vote. District staff may advocate on their own
time with their own resources. They may not campaign on duty and may
not use district email, websites, or accounts for it. The district can
publish a balanced factual summary and nothing more. So:

- Teacher videos are filmed off school grounds or outside contract
  hours, framed as personal opinion, with no district logos.
- Nobody asks LPS staff to share committee content from a school
  account or during the workday. Give every teacher a one-page card of
  what they can and can't do. It protects them.
- The district's factual budget pages are safe to link. The advocacy
  is ours alone.

**Consent.** Signed media releases for every identifiable adult. A
parent's signed consent for any minor, kept by the committee. No
student is ever filmed at school for committee content.

## 7. Accessibility

Seniors are a core audience. Accessible content is also the content
that gets shared.

- Alt text on every image. Describe what's in it and any words on it.
- Captions on every video. Most people watch muted. Fix the auto
  captions by hand (names, dollar figures). Burn them in for Reels.
- CamelCase the hashtags: #YesOnLPS. Screen readers can't parse
  #yesonlps.
- Text in graphics at 24px or larger, AA contrast, and never put the
  tax math only in an image. Repeat the numbers in the post text.
- No emoji as bullets. No decorative Unicode fonts.
- One home for everything: citizensforlps.org. Every post, every ad,
  every volunteer share links there.
- One logo, one palette, one type treatment, with the disclaimer built
  into the templates.

## 8. Campaigns that won, and what they did

| Campaign | What they did | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Elk River, MN (2019, $223M bond) | A debunking hub on the website; supporters inside private Facebook groups; traced misinformation to its sources, half out of state | Passed |
| Chapel Hill ISD, TX (2023, $113.9M) | One landing page linked from everything | All three propositions passed |
| Spring ISD, TX (2022, $850M) | A student-narrated explainer of how bonds work | All three passed |
| Athens SD, WI (2023) | Carousels breaking down the taxpayer impact | 74% support |
| Mustang PS, OK | Answered nearly every comment; 70+ public meetings | Passed |
| Morton SD, WA ($24.5M) | Positive, everyday school content all year before the ask | Passed |
| ESD 112 (WA) | Research first, then phased communication | 94% pass rate across bonds and levies |

Denver Public Schools and Douglas County both have overrides on the
same ballot. Watch their pages weekly. Borrow what works. Watch for
attack lines that migrate.

## 9. Week by week

Every week: Monday is the math or a fact. Wednesday is a person on
video. Friday is an endorsement or a community post. Sunday the share
kit goes out. Comment coverage and rapid response run every day.

### Before ballots (Sept 1 to Oct 1)

**Week 1 (Sept 1 to 7).** Start the Meta authorization with two
admins. Claim and audit the handles. Create the Nextdoor group. Make
sure citizensforlps.org has the social links, the disclaimer, the FAQ,
and the tax table. Launch post: who we are and why, co-chairs, 45
seconds. Publish the moderation policy. Start recruiting volunteers
from the form. Build the WhatsApp tree and the template kit. Get the
TABOR FOR statement written and submitted before the ~Sept 18 deadline.

**Week 2 (Sept 8 to 14).** The story of the $10.6 million gap, the
furlough day, and the wage freeze, told by a parent. Link the district's
budget page for the facts. First myth-vs-fact card: where the money
goes. First Nextdoor introductions, one per neighborhood. First film
day: six to eight testimonials in a batch. Yard signs and postcards
start going out Saturday the 12th. The first two district forums are
the 14th and 15th. Invite people.

**Week 3 (Sept 15 to 21).** The math week. The ladder graphic and the
calculator. Testimonial: a veteran teacher. Card: central admin is
under 2%. First paid flight if the authorization cleared, $50 to $75 a
day on video views.

**Week 4 (Sept 22 to 28).** The money stays local. A 30-second
explainer. Testimonial: a grandparent on home values. First
endorsement batch. First live Q&A. Build the profile-photo overlay.
Forums on the 23rd and 28th.

**Week 5 (Sept 29 to Oct 1).** The fiscal-conservative testimonial.
Endorsement roll. Card: "they'll just ask again" and enrollment. Three
forums this week (29th, 30th, Oct 1); post what people asked and how
the district answered. Every October graphic drafted and approved:
drop-box map, countdown set, "ballots are here" script. Stage the
"your ballot is coming" explainer with what the measure looks like on
the ballot.

### Ballots out (Oct 2 to 26)

**Week 6 (Oct 2 to 8).** Ballots start mailing Friday the 2nd. Profile
photo day. Daily posting begins. Hero post: check your mailbox, find
the LPS measure, vote yes. Envelope selfie chain (#YesOnLPS). Re-cut
the best testimonials with a "ballots are out, vote today" end card.
Paid shifts to vote-now creative and retargeting. Volunteer ask one:
text five friends, "did your ballot come?" Nextdoor round two: one
"ballots are here, happy to answer questions" post per neighborhood.
Lit-drop walks on the 3rd and 4th.

**Week 7 (Oct 9 to 15).** "Why I already voted yes" wave from
volunteers. Endorsement recap carousel. Second live Q&A: ask us
anything before you vote. Lit-drop walks on the 10th and 11th.

**Week 8 (Oct 16 to 22).** Keep the pressure steady. Voter service
centers open the 19th; point anyone with a lost ballot there. Best
performers re-boosted. Volunteer ask two: "have you sent it back?"

**Week 9 (Oct 23 to 26).** By the 24th, every final-week ad is live at
a low budget. Oct 26 is the last day to register for a mailed ballot
and the last day we tell anyone to mail one. Say both, loudly.

### The chase (Oct 27 to Nov 3)

**Oct 27 to Nov 2.** Drop boxes only. Two posts a day: countdown
("7 days, ballots must be received by 7 p.m. Nov 3"), the drop-box
map, fifteen-second urgency clips from the best messengers. Scale
budgets on the running ads. Volunteers text everyone who hasn't
confirmed. Stories: "drive with me to the drop box." Rapid response on
alert; last-minute attacks are why the blackout exists.

**Election Day, Tuesday Nov 3.** Morning: today, drop box by 7 p.m.,
mail is too late. Again at noon and 4 p.m. with the nearest-box finder.
Stories every two hours. At 7 p.m., stop. That night, a thank-you post
drafted in advance for both outcomes. The next day, archive everything.
The next campaign starts with this document.
