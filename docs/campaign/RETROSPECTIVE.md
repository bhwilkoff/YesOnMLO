# What happened, and what we learned

Written November 5, 2026, two days after the election, while we still remember it.

## The result

**4A, the LPS mill levy override, passed.** At our last check it had 23,846 yes
and 19,429 no, or 55.1%. Those numbers are unofficial, from November 4 at
5:26 p.m., and ballots were still being counted. The district announced the
win that afternoon.

That was a harder year than the number looks:

- 55.1% is lower than the 56.8% to 60.3% that LPS earned from 2010 to 2020.
- Next door, Jeffco's two overrides and Douglas County's were all trailing on
  November 4. Mesa County Valley District 51's measure was far behind.
- Of 37 school questions we found on Colorado ballots, 23 were ahead on
  November 4. So a strong district couldn't assume anything.

## What we built

This site, a free storytelling toolkit for volunteers, and an Instagram account
started from zero on September 23. The official campaign site stayed the home for
voters. We ran the social team from a single posting document.

## Strategy

These were private until the election ended. Decision 063 explains why we're
publishing them now.

**Who we were talking to**

- **Persuasion:** swing and conservative-leaning suburban voters. We led with
  local control, dollars staying in LPS classrooms, accountability and keeping
  teachers, not the abstract idea of funding schools.
- **Base:** parents, staff and education-first voters. We mobilized them and
  didn't spend time re-persuading them.
- **Watch:** seniors and homeowners without kids in school. Clear tax math and
  property values mattered most here.

**Message**

- Be specific. "Local money that stays in LPS" landed with more conservative-leaning
  voters.
- Avoid "Littleton" alone. The district includes part of Centennial, and those
  voters count.
- A tagline needs to be readable at driving speed, and clearly yours. Statewide
  measures and other districts were all on the same ballot.
- Say the real cost. The district's number was about $25 a year per $100,000 of
  home value, and we showed the math.
- Say what a no means, in the district's own words, without threats.

**Timing**

Everything hangs on when ballots are mailed. In a vote-by-mail state, Election Day
is a month long, and persuasion has to be mostly done when ballots land. After that
the job is getting ballots back. Late on, that meant drop boxes, since the mail was
too slow.

## What worked

**Painted cars.** Painted car windows did a lot of work for us. They cost almost
nothing, they moved around town on their own, and they made the news on CBS
Colorado.

**Nostalgia.** Throwback posts that got parents remembering their own time in LPS
did very well. The Arapahoe 1993 Reel reached about 10,000 views on Facebook. But
it only works when the post ties back to the vote on the image itself. Pictures
that were merely old got nowhere with us, and we threw some out.

**Native posts over links.** On our Facebook page's history from earlier campaigns,
short warm posts and results drew 20 to 74 reactions, while link shares drew 5 to
14. So we led with images and kept links out of the way.

**A one-stop shop.** Tables outside the district's budget forums let people get a
car painted, take a sign, write postcards and pick up a lit-drop route all at once.

**Handwritten letters and postcards.** Neighbors writing to neighbors, at kitchen
tables. They also made good pictures, once addresses were blurred.

**One simple posting doc.** One document, each day's post in order, with the exact
caption and a numbered folder of files. Not a strategy essay. A busy volunteer needs
to be able to just post.

**Amplification.** Getting a crew whose only job was to hype our posts was worth as
much as the posts themselves.

**Working in public.** This repo stayed public. A campaign about trust shouldn't
hide how it works.

## What we'd change

**Start with more admins.** Getting several people onto Facebook and Instagram was
essential and much harder than it sounds. We should have run tutorials for all of
them early. We also lost access to an old Instagram account and had to start over.

**Watch your paid ads.** Facebook and Instagram ads got lots of eyeballs, including
bots and people outside our community. Turn comments off on all ads, and target
as tightly as you can.

**Collect pictures from the start.** Pictures and video are currency. Ask for them
at every event and send them to one person. We ended up with six photos from our
own election night party.

**Make permissions a habit.** Get photo releases as you go, and ask before you
use footage. Decide early how you'll handle kids. We covered faces with emoji on some
posts, which is clumsy.

**Check every claim on the real page.** We almost promised "get a yard sign" when
the real thing was a checkbox on a volunteer form. Go find the button first.

## Lessons for the content itself

These came from real corrections during the campaign. Details are in
`MEDIA-PLAYBOOK.md`.

- A Reel has one premise, and every shot pays it off. Cut the cute shot that doesn't.
- Don't repeat a subject from a recent post. Followers notice.
- A caption should say something new, not describe the picture.
- Never tell alumni how old they are.
- Keep it true. Nothing that reads as AI-made, no stock smiles. Real photos,
  unfiltered, in the platform's own plain text style.
- Sound like the page. Ours was short, warm and grateful.

## What a machine got wrong

We used an AI assistant heavily, and it was a big help. It also made real mistakes,
all caught by looking:

- It built a whole concept around a school concert because a video was timestamped
  7:00 p.m. on election night. The concert had nothing to do with the campaign.
- It called trays of 3D-printed sheep campaign buttons, from a tiny thumbnail.
- It used raw photos that showed a voter's name and address on an envelope, in
  a draft Reel. A frame check caught it before posting.
- It read the countywide vote gap as if it were ours, until a volunteer pointed out
  that most of the county can't vote on our measure.

So, if you use one, a person has to look at everything before it goes out. It's
a fast helper and a poor judge of what's true about your community.
