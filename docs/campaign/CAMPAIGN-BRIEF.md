## Why the district says it's needed

- **$10.6M structural budget deficit**, driven by state-level revenue
  constraints, shifts in Colorado's school funding formula, declining
  enrollment, and rising operational costs.
- The 2026–27 budget was balanced with **one-time measures**: ~$1.1M
  in staffing reductions, ~$5.4M from the general fund, an employee
  **wage freeze**, and a scheduled districtwide **furlough day**.
- Without ongoing local funding, the district faces deeper, permanent
  cuts.

Source: LPS press release 2026-08-14; LRPC minutes 2026-07-29.

## What the MLO funds (the three pillars)

1. **Attracting and retaining outstanding teachers** — lifts the wage
   freeze, restores step advancements, keeps compensation competitive.
2. **Sustaining academic excellence and career readiness** — protects
   classroom funding, keeps baseline student-to-teacher ratios,
   preserves career-readiness programming.
3. **Maintaining safe, secure schools and operations** — safety,
   wellness, and operational needs; prevents future furlough days.

If voters approve: negotiated compensation is reinstated
**retroactively** to the start of the contract year, and the spring
furlough day is removed from the calendar.

LPS voters have approved every recent funding measure by 13+ points:

| 2013 | Bond | $80M | **60.34%** | 39.66% | 33,213 |
| 2018 | Bond (50-Year Plan) | $298.87M | **56.83%** | 43.17% | 53,643 |
| 2020 | MLO (6→11 mills, Debt-Free Schools Act) | — | **57.66%** | 42.34% | 62,581 |

Source: LPS LRPC Meeting #6 deck (July 29, 2026), "Recent Bond/Mill
Levy Information" slide.

Working read: the district's floor in even-year, high-turnout
elections has been ~57%. The 2026 headwinds (property-tax fatigue,
statewide Prop NN on the same ballot, many districts running MLOs at
once) mean the campaign treats that floor as earned, not guaranteed.
